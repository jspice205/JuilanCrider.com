Same house, lower payment: buydowns and seller credits

When sellers want to get a deal done, a credit toward your costs can be worth more to you than a lower price. Used well, it lowers your monthly payment. Here’s how it works in plain English.

What is a seller credit?

It’s money the seller puts toward your costs at closing. It’s negotiated as part of your offer, and your loan program sets a limit on how much you can receive.

Temporary buydown

The credit lowers your interest rate for the first year or two (a 2-1 buydown is common). Your payment steps up to the full rate afterward. It helps if you expect your income to rise or plan to refinance.

Permanent buydown

Paying “points” up front lowers your rate for the life of the loan. It makes the most sense if you plan to keep the loan for years.

Which one fits you?

It depends on your budget and how long you’ll keep the loan. Always have your lender run both side by side before you write the offer, and I’ll help you decide how to ask for it.
