Thanksgiving week in Las Vegas: free help, holiday lights and one thing worth sharing

Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. Whether you’re hosting twenty people or keeping it small, here are six things happening this week in and around Las Vegas, almost all of them free, plus the times already converted to Pacific so you don’t have to do the math at 6 a.m. with a frozen bird.

1. Free turkey experts you can call or text

The Butterball Turkey Talk-Line is staffed by real people and it’s free. Call 1-800-288-8372 or text 844-877-3456. On Thanksgiving Day it’s open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Eastern, which is 4 a.m. to 4 p.m. here in Las Vegas, so call early.

2. The USDA food safety hotline

Not sure if the turkey thawed long enough, or how long leftovers are safe? The USDA Meat and Poultry Hotline is free at 1-888-674-6854. On Thanksgiving Day it’s open 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. Eastern, which is 5 a.m. to 11 a.m. Pacific.

3. A free holiday walk after dinner

Bellagio’s Conservatory has opened its winter display, “Postcards From Christmas,” and it’s free to walk through, with no tickets and no reservations. It’s a nice way to walk off dinner and get the whole family out of the house.

4. One more holiday light show, almost free

The Ethel M Holiday Cactus Garden in Henderson is lit up through January 3, from 5 to 10 p.m. most nights. Last season admission was $3 per person, with kids under 2 and seniors 65 and up free. Check ethelm.com for this year’s price before you go.

5. The one kitchen rule worth repeating

According to the National Fire Protection Association, Thanksgiving is the number one day of the year for home cooking fires, and unattended cooking is the leading cause. Stay in the kitchen while something is on the stove, keep a lid nearby to smother a pan fire, and test your smoke alarm before guests arrive.

6. Something to share with someone who could use it

The City of North Las Vegas is holding its annual turkey giveaway and resource fair on Saturday, November 21, starting at 8 a.m. at Liberty Park by North Las Vegas City Hall. Turkeys and bread go to the first 500 households while supplies last. If you know a family who could use a little help this year, forward them this. Check the city’s page for any last-minute changes.

A bonus for homeowners

While the family is in town and the paperwork talk comes up: if you own your home in Clark County and live in it, make sure it’s claimed as your primary residence so your property tax bill gets the 3% cap. Here’s the 2-minute explainer: Nevada’s 3% property tax cap.
