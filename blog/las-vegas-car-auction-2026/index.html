A record Ferrari sale at Wynn, and what it says about Las Vegas luxury

On October 31, Broad Arrow held its second annual Las Vegas auction at Wynn Las Vegas, as part of the Concours at Wynn weekend. The next day, the Concours team posted a carousel of the standout sales on Instagram. Swipe through it below, then here’s what sold, and why I think it matters if you’re shopping for a luxury home in Las Vegas.

The headline sales

A 1997 Ferrari F50 with just 106 miles on it sold for a reported $17 million. Broad Arrow had estimated it at more than $20 million, so it came in under the estimate. It still set a new record for the model, beating the $14.575 million paid for an F50 at Mecum’s Monterey sale in August.

The top price of the night was a reported $18.5 million for a one-of-a-kind Ferrari Daytona SP3. The 86-car catalog also included a 2003 Ferrari Enzo, a 1971 Lamborghini Miura P400 SV and a 2023 Bugatti Chiron Super Sport, each estimated in the millions.

For scale, last year’s first Las Vegas sale totaled about $13.7 million for the whole auction, and its top car, a Bugatti Veyron, brought $2.2 million. This year, a single car came close to the entire 2025 sale.

Las Vegas is now a collector-car city

Between the Concours at Wynn, the Tour d’Elegance on the Strip and the SEMA Show this week, early November puts some of the world’s most valuable cars in one place. Collectors fly in for it, and many of them start asking what it would be like to live here: no state income tax, an easy flight from most of the West, and homes built with room for a serious collection.

What a car auction teaches about pricing a home

The F50 is a good lesson. It set a record, and it still sold below its estimate. Rarity and condition got it a record price, and the market still decided what that price was. Luxury homes work the same way. A one-of-a-kind estate in The Summit or MacDonald Highlands can set a record and still fall short of an ambitious list price. The sellers who do best price on real comparable sales, not on hope.

If you’re bringing a collection

More of my out-of-state clients ask about the garage before the kitchen. In Las Vegas and Henderson, that can mean climate-controlled bays, car lifts, a gallery garage you can see from the living room, RV or trailer parking, and a community whose HOA rules actually allow it. Not every luxury neighborhood allows all of that, so it’s worth checking the CC&Rs before you fall for a house.
