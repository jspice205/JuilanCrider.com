Moving to Las Vegas: your Nevada driver’s license and car registration, step by step

You closed on the house, the boxes are in, and somewhere in the pile is a reminder that Nevada wants your license and your car too. The good news: it’s a short list. The catch: the clock starts when you become a resident, and it’s only 30 days.

The 30-day rule

The Nevada DMV says drivers moving in from another state must get a Nevada license within 30 days of becoming a resident. Your car has the same window: register it within 30 days of establishing residency, or when you get your Nevada license, whichever comes first.

Step 1: Gather proof that you live here

For the license, the DMV asks for two documents that show your Nevada address, things like a utility bill, a mortgage statement, a rental agreement or a bank statement. If you just bought, your closing paperwork and your first utility bill usually come first. You’ll also turn in your out-of-state license when you get the Nevada one.

Step 2: Switch your insurance to a Nevada policy

This is the step people miss. To register the car, the DMV requires a Nevada insurance card from a company licensed in Nevada. An out-of-state policy isn’t accepted, so call your insurer before your DMV visit.

Step 3: Get the car ready

For an out-of-state car, the DMV lists a VIN inspection (done at a DMV office, an authorized DMV agency or by a law enforcement officer), your title or current registration, and your old plates. If you live in the Las Vegas Valley, most gas cars also need a smog check before registration. The DMV keeps the testing map and the exemptions on its emissions page, so check yours there.

Step 4: Book it as one trip

Even if you use the DMV’s online tools, new residents who haven’t swapped their out-of-state license yet still need an in-person visit. Make an appointment, bring the license paperwork and the car paperwork together, and knock out both in one visit.

The takeaway

Order matters: Nevada insurance first, then smog and VIN, then one DMV visit for both the license and the registration. Put a reminder on your calendar for day 20 so the 30 days don’t sneak up on you.
